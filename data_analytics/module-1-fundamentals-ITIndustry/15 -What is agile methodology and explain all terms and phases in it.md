# What is Agile methodology?

Agile methodology is a way of developing software and managing projects in small, iterative steps.

Instead of completing the whole project at once, Agile focuses on delivering small working parts of the project quickly and improving them based on feedback.

It is a flexible and customer-focused approach that allows teams to adapt to changing requirements.

# Why Agile is used

Agile is used because it helps teams:
- respond quickly to changes
- deliver value early
- improve collaboration
- reduce project risk
- get feedback from customers often

# Core principles of Agile

The Agile manifesto is based on the following principles:

- customer satisfaction is the highest priority
- welcome changing requirements even late in development
- deliver working software frequently
- business people and developers must work together daily
- build projects around motivated individuals
- face-to-face communication is effective
- working software is the main measure of progress
- sustainable development pace
- continuous attention to technical excellence
- simplicity is essential
- best designs emerge from self-organized teams
- regular reflection and improvement

# Important Agile terms

## 1. Sprint

A sprint is a short time-boxed period in which a specific set of work is completed.

Typical sprint duration:
- 1 to 4 weeks

## 2. Product Backlog

A product backlog is a prioritized list of features, tasks, and requirements for the product.

## 3. Sprint Backlog

A sprint backlog is the list of tasks selected from the product backlog to be completed in the current sprint.

## 4. User Story

A user story is a short description of a feature from the perspective of the end user.

Example:

As a customer, I want to log in so that I can access my account.

## 5. Scrum

Scrum is one of the most common Agile frameworks.

It includes roles like:
- Product Owner
- Scrum Master
- Development Team

## 6. Product Owner

The Product Owner is responsible for deciding what work should be done first.

They manage the product backlog and prioritize features based on business value.

## 7. Scrum Master

The Scrum Master helps the team follow Agile practices and removes obstacles that block progress.

## 8. Development Team

The development team is responsible for building the product and completing sprint tasks.

## 9. Daily Stand-up

A daily stand-up is a short daily meeting where the team discusses:
- what they did yesterday
- what they will do today
- what is blocking them

## 10. Retrospective

A retrospective is a meeting at the end of a sprint where the team reviews what went well and what should improve.

## 11. Velocity

Velocity is the amount of work a team completes in a sprint.

It helps estimate and plan future sprints.

## 12. Burndown Chart

A burndown chart shows remaining work over time during a sprint.

## 13. Increment

An increment is the completed part of the product delivered at the end of a sprint.

# Agile phases

Agile is usually performed in cycles or phases. The major phases are:

1. Planning
2. Design
3. Development
4. Testing
5. Review and Feedback
6. Deployment
7. Retrospective

## 1. Planning

The team identifies the project goal and decides what to build in the next sprint.

This includes:
- setting priorities
- breaking work into tasks
- estimating effort

## 2. Design

The team creates the initial design for the selected features.

This includes:
- user interface design
- architecture planning
- workflow design

## 3. Development

The team builds the product in small increments.

The work is divided into modules or user stories and developed in short cycles.

## 4. Testing

During testing, the team checks whether the product works correctly and meets the expected quality.

Testing includes:
- unit testing
- integration testing
- regression testing

## 5. Review and Feedback

The team reviews the completed work with stakeholders and customers.

Feedback is collected and used to improve the next sprint.

## 6. Deployment

The working product is released to the customer or production environment.

## 7. Retrospective

At the end of the sprint, the team reflects on:
- what was successful
- what did not work
- how to improve next time

# Agile workflow example

```text
Product Backlog --> Sprint Planning --> Design --> Development --> Testing --> Review --> Deployment --> Retrospective
```

# Advantages of Agile methodology

- flexible and adaptive
- faster delivery
- continuous customer feedback
- better team collaboration
- reduces project risk
- allows changes in requirements

# Disadvantages of Agile methodology

- requires active customer involvement
- can be difficult for large teams
- unclear scope can affect planning
- frequent changes may cause confusion

# Summary

Agile methodology is a flexible and iterative approach to software development.

It focuses on delivering small working parts of the project quickly and improving them through customer feedback.

Important Agile terms include sprint, product backlog, sprint backlog, user story, Scrum, Product Owner, Scrum Master, and daily stand-up.

The main Agile phases include planning, design, development, testing, review, deployment, and retrospective.

# Final thought

Agile methodology is widely used in modern software development because it helps teams work faster, adapt to change, and deliver better products based on real customer feedback.
